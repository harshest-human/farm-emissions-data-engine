Hello, I am Harsh.

I follow emissions from the barn to the report.

I am an analytics-automation scientist and PhD researcher. I combine livestock science, analytical instruments, IoT sensors and reproducible data to support a healthier, more resilient food system through One Health.

I currently live in Hamburg. I work at Leibniz ATB in Potsdam and pursue my PhD at Freie Universität Berlin. These are notes from my work and the people I meet along the way.

What I did · what I do · what I want to build

Then

I began with food.

I studied Dairy and Food Technology, tested milk and food for contaminants and adulteration, then moved upstream at Kiel University into feed analytics, ruminant nutrition and precision livestock farming.

Now

I measure the barn.

In my PhD, I build 24/7 measurement arrays and automated pipelines for CH₄ and NH₃. I work with FTIR, CRDS, TDLAS, PAS and smaller NDIR, electrochemical and MOS sensors, supported by SOPs and QA.

Next

I want the evidence to scale.

I am building automated dMRV workflows and APIs that connect sensors with GHG and NH₃ calculations—from housing and farm level to Scope 3 and national use.

My goal is to make documentation simpler and more traceable for EU CSRD and ESRS E1, the Carbon Removals and Carbon Farming framework (CRCF), SBTi FLAG and independent assurance—without treating software as certification.

What I am doing now · EmiMod

I help improve livestock-emission measurements across Germany.

I work in the nationwide EmiMod project with research institutes, universities and public bodies. I contribute to three work packages:

  • WP 4 — I develop automated IoT sensor systems for scalable emission measurements.
  • WP 6 — I support quality assurance of reference methods for CH₄ and NH₃ from dairy barns.
  • WP 10 — I develop predictive and advanced statistical models for emissions.

Field journal

Work, people and moments I remember.

Field campaign · LVAT Groß Kreutz

I learn the most when methods meet the same barn.

I joined an industrial round-robin experiment with EmiMod partners and DAkkS-accredited measurement institutes. We compared systems under the same field conditions and worked toward measurements that others can trust.

RAMIRAN · Wageningen · 2025

I presented what sampling position can do to an emission result.

I analysed thousands of observations from a naturally ventilated dairy barn. I shared how spatial variation affects reported CH₄, NH₃ and CO₂—and enjoyed the discussion with colleagues afterwards.

CIGR · 2026

I use conferences to test ideas, not only to present them.

I presented our EmiMod work, acknowledged the team behind it and listened carefully to questions. Good questions usually show me where the next analysis should begin.

Building a measurement system

Sometimes research looks like carrying a great many tubes.

I build the physical path from barn air to analyser, then automate the data path that follows it. I work with FTIR, CRDS, TDLAS and PAS reference instruments as well as NDIR, electrochemical and MOS sensors.

Grüne Woche · Berlin · 2025

I enjoy making invisible gases understandable.

I demonstrated our low-cost sensors at Grüne Woche. Public conversations remind me that a useful measurement must also be explainable.

European exchange · Germany 🇩🇪 · EU 🇪🇺 · Netherlands 🇳🇱

I want emission methods to travel well across borders.

I hosted colleagues from Wageningen University & Research, showed them our facilities and discussed how we can harmonise measurements across the EU. I value the network I have built within Germany and across Europe.

European cooperation

I saw how scientific exchange can connect research and policy.

I took part in an ATB exchange with Dutch parliamentarian Silvio Erkens. We discussed cooperation for sustainable and resilient agriculture in Europe.

KTBL emissions conference · Bonn · 2023

I began sharing this work while the questions were still taking shape.

I presented my research at the KTBL emissions conference and learned how measurement science connects with methods used by farms and public institutions.

EuroTier · Hannover · 2022

I brought our research into an industry conversation.

I represented ATB at the BLE stand and listened to the practical questions behind new livestock technologies. Those conversations still shape how I think about scalable tools.

Leadership · EurAgEng YPN

I create spaces where young researchers can be heard.

As Chair of the EurAgEng Young Professionals Network, I organise sessions, moderate discussions and build relationships with industry. I coordinated sponsorship with Agreenculture for a young-researcher poster award.

PhD community · 2023–2025

I learned that research culture also needs care.

My colleagues elected me as PhD representative for three consecutive years. I organised workshops and mini-conferences, represented their concerns and made time for the informal moments that hold a community together.

Freie Universität Berlin · AI workshop

I explored what an agricultural idea could become.

I presented a prototype startup idea at a workshop on using artificial intelligence for a better tomorrow. It helped me connect research code with the needs of future users.

My research home

I do this work with people, never alone.

I rely on engineers, technicians, scientists, students and farm teams. I train colleagues, write SOPs and coordinate campaigns, but I also learn from everyone who enters the measurement container.

Things I am building

I use open code to organise what I learn.

Farm Emissions Data Engine — I am building a reproducible R and Python path from raw analyser, weather, barn-climate and animal data to clean, traceable, KTBL-oriented datasets.

Livestock Emissions dMRV — I am exploring a sensor-informed Python workflow for housing-level methane measurement, reporting and verification.

I am also interested in how farm evidence can support Scope 3, CSRD/ESRS E1, SBTi FLAG and EU carbon-farming workflows. I focus on the data trail; independent bodies remain responsible for assurance, validation and verification.
